The default mindset

Make Aperture Priority your home base

Put shutter speed on A. Put ISO on A. Choose the aperture with the lens ring. Now you decide depth of field; the camera chooses shutter speed and ISO within the guardrails you set.

Shutter dialACamera chooses shutter speed.
ISO dialAAuto ISO raises sensitivity when needed.
Aperture ringYou choosef/2 for separation • f/4–5.6 general • f/8 for depth.
Exposure comp.📍 Top-right · #20 to startThen bias brighter (+) or darker (–) by eye.
Why this works: the X100VI has one fixed 23mm f/2 lens (35mm-equivalent view), so aperture is your quickest creative control. Auto ISO is not “giving up”; it lets you protect shutter speed while you concentrate on timing, framing and light.

Set up three useful Auto ISO banks once

BankUse it forSuggested starting point

The camera raises ISO when light would otherwise force shutter speed below your chosen minimum. In very low light it can still go slower after reaching the maximum ISO, so keep an eye on shutter speed.

Where is that control?

X100VI visual control map

These are the controls you will touch most often in this guide. Learn their physical location first; the menu names will make more sense afterward.

Top + front: exposure and focus

1
Shutter speed / ISO dial
Top-left. Rotate for shutter speed; lift the outer dial and rotate to choose ISO. For our normal workflow, both usually sit at A.
2
Exposure compensation dial
Top-right. Your “make the picture brighter/darker” control when using automatic exposure.
3
Aperture ring
Around the lens. This is the control I want your left hand to learn by feel: f/2, f/2.8, f/4, f/5.6, f/8…
4
Focus-mode selector
Front-left side of the camera body. S = single AF, C = continuous AF, M = manual focus.
5
Shutter release
Half-press meters/focuses; full press makes the photograph. Build the habit of a deliberate half-press rather than stabbing the button.

Rear: navigation, focus point and fast settings

6
Rear command dial
Upper-right rear. Rotate it to change a highlighted Q-menu value and to resize the focus box; press it for its assigned function.
7
Q button
Rear-right. Opens your Quick Menu. This becomes your field dashboard once we customize it.
8
Focus stick / joystick
Move the active focus point without taking your eye far from the scene. Also used for menu navigation.
9
MENU/OK
For deeper settings. Ideally you use this less while shooting; our Q Menu and custom banks should keep you out of here.
Muscle-memory goal: without looking, your right hand should find exposure compensation, Q and the focus stick; your left hand should find aperture and the focus-mode selector.

Photography, not menuology

The exposure triangle in plain English

Aperture

What it changes: depth of field + light.

f/2 = more light, blurrier background.
f/8 = less light, more front-to-back sharpness.

Shutter

What it changes: motion + light.

1/1000 = freezes motion.
1/30 = allows blur and camera movement.

ISO

What it changes: sensitivity + image noise.

Low ISO = cleaner file.
High ISO = lets you keep faster shutters in dim light.

Priority order for handheld people photography: 1) enough shutter speed to stop motion, 2) aperture for the look you want, 3) let ISO become whatever it needs to be. Noise is usually fixable; motion blur usually is not.
The clever Fuji stuff

What the controls actually do

Q
Quick Menu = your dashboard. Press Q for frequently changed settings. Highlight one with the focus stick and turn the rear command dial. Long-press Q to edit the menu/custom setting workflow.
±
Exposure Compensation = “I want this brighter/darker.” It biases the camera’s automatic exposure without forcing you into full manual. Use it constantly; it is one of the most photographic controls on the camera.
AF
AF-S = focus, then stop. Best for subjects whose distance is not changing much. AF-C = keep updating. Best when subject distance is changing.
▦
AF area = where the camera is allowed to hunt. Single Point gives precision. Zone gives moving subjects some room. Wide/Tracking gives the camera more control.
ND
ND filter = 4-stop built-in sunglasses. Use it when you want f/2 in bright sun or intentional slow shutter in daylight. It changes exposure, not the “film look.”
DR
Dynamic Range = highlight insurance. DR200/400 can protect bright tones by requiring higher ISO and altering tonal processing. Useful in hard sun; not something you need to micromanage every frame.
FS
Film Simulation = the color/tonal personality. PROVIA is neutral, Velvia vivid, ASTIA softer, Classic Chrome restrained/documentary, Classic Neg. punchier nostalgia, REALA ACE natural with character, ETERNA subdued/cinematic, ACROS monochrome.
R
Recipe = Film Simulation plus supporting ingredients. White balance/shift, tone curve, color, grain, Color Chrome effects, sharpness, noise reduction and clarity can all reshape the JPEG. A recipe is a starting aesthetic—not a technical requirement.
Make the camera yours

Use C1–C7 as seven “looks,” not seven complicated cameras

The X100VI can recall seven custom banks. My recommendation: keep the shooting behavior mostly consistent and let the banks primarily hold JPEG looks/recipes. That way switching from “Reala Everyday” to “Classic Chrome Documentary” does not unexpectedly wreck your autofocus strategy.

When a photo is going wrong

10-second rescue checklist

1
Blurry person? Shutter is too slow → choose AUTO3 / raise shutter speed / open aperture.
2
Wrong thing sharp? Reduce AF area → use Single Point or smaller Zone.
3
Moving subject keeps escaping focus? Switch AF-S → AF-C and use Zone/Wide.
4
Photo too dark/bright even though exposure is “correct”? Turn the exposure-compensation dial. Trust the picture, not zero.
5
Can’t use f/2 in bright sun? Turn ND filter ON.
6
Too much menu confusion? Return to: shutter A • ISO A • aperture f/4 • AF-S • REALA ACE • exposure comp 0. Then solve one problem at a time.
A simple practice plan

Four outings to become faster

Outing 1 — Aperture: photograph the same subject at f/2, f/4 and f/8. Notice background and depth.

Outing 2 — Motion: photograph walkers/cars at 1/60, 1/250 and 1/1000. Notice when motion freezes.

Outing 3 — Exposure compensation: photograph a bright scene at –1, 0 and +1 EV. Decide which feels right.

Outing 4 — One-look walk: choose Classic Chrome or ACROS and leave it there. Spend the outing thinking only about light, timing and framing.
